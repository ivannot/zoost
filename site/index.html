You built it. It is yours.
And the platform gives you no way to see it whole.

Zoost is a small family of Chrome extensions, one per Zoho product. Each mirrors what you have built inside that product — code, schema, relations, dependencies — into plain files on your own disk, then layers navigation, diagrams, search, audit and export on top of that mirror. None of them calls a write endpoint: they read definitions and metadata, never records. Everything runs in your browser.

Free, open source, and each with a written guide. Both are on the Chrome Web Store. Zoost CRM is on the Chrome Web Store; Zoost Analytics is submitted and in review.

What every Zoost has in common

Read-only, on purpose

No Zoost calls an endpoint that creates, edits or deletes anything in Zoho — not a function, not a view, not a setting. That is a property of which endpoints we call, not something the browser enforces, and every call either one can make is in about twenty readable files. Zoho compiles and validates server-side, and owning a write path would mean owning deployment, conflicts and rollback — a different product. You keep working where you already work.

Plain files, your Git

The mirror is ordinary files in a folder you pick: source, JSON, SQL. Commit it and git diff answers "what changed last month" — a question neither product can answer about itself.

The whole thing at once

Both platforms show you one object at a time. Both Zoosts show you the shape: what depends on what, what nothing depends on, where a name is referenced, and one document you can hand to someone who does not have the extension.

Numbers, never verdicts

Counts and lists, with no thresholds and no score. A long function may be a clean mapping table; a view nothing reads may feed a scheduled export. Every figure says what it does not cover, right next to itself, and the judgement stays yours.

Nothing leaves the machine

No server, no account, no telemetry. The extensions use the Zoho session you are already signed into and write to a folder you choose. The one exception is the optional AI assistant — and only if you configure it, with your own key, sending only to the provider you picked.

One interface, learned once

They are twins. The workspace bar, the environment guard, the dialogs, the button colours, the exports and the diagram controls are the same thing in both, down to the wording. Use one and you already know the other.

Certain, or stopped

No loops against a guess, no matching a localised button label, no synthetic clicks into a page we do not own. Where a path is not certain, the feature stops with a precise message. The exceptions are narrow, deliberate and written down: a bounded wait for a known element, one retry on a genuinely transient failure, and recovering by an action that was measured to work — the CSRF token is read from a known list of cookies rather than assumed. What a Zoost cannot know, it says it cannot know.

Checkable, not just claimed

Releases are built by GitHub from a tagged commit, twice over to prove the bytes are reproducible, and signed with a provenance statement. The archive on the Release is the file submitted to the Store — and one command tells you whether that is true, without taking our word for it. The record of every submitted version is in RELEASES.md, the build is short and does nothing clever, and llms.txt maps the whole chain for anyone — or anything — assessing it.

Open, and yours to fork

Apache-2.0, no build step, no dependencies, no framework. The source is readable by anyone about to give it access to their data — which is the point, since that is exactly what you are being asked to do.

Built and maintained by one person, with substantial help from Claude — “Claudio”, to me — on design, code and wording. The judgement calls, and the responsibility for the result, are mine.

Get started

  1. Pick the product you want to look at: Zoost — workbench for Zoho CRM or Zoost — workbench for Zoho Analytics.
  2. Install the extension — from the Chrome Web Store for Zoho CRM. Zoost Analytics is submitted and in review; until it is published you can load it unpacked from source.
  3. Choose one working folder. Each Zoost keeps its own subfolder inside it, so a single folder serves them all and the two never meet.
  4. Open the Zoho tab you want to mirror, create the workspace and pull. Then git init, and you have a history from today onwards.

No Zoost calls an endpoint that writes to Zoho, so the worst it can do to your data is nothing — a property of which endpoints we call, and every one of them is in files you can read.
